Chadar — Frozen River Trek

There's no trail here — the route is the river itself, frozen into a shifting sheet of ice that trekkers have walked on for generations to reach isolated Zanskari villages. It's one of the most extreme and unusual treks in the world, done only in the depths of winter.

Overview

What this trek is actually like

"Chadar" means sheet, referring to the ice sheet that forms over the Zanskar river each winter, deep enough in places to walk on. For centuries this frozen river was the only winter link between Zanskar villages and Leh — traders and school-going children still occasionally use sections of it.

You're not trekking a marked path; you're following the ice itself, which shifts and cracks through the season, meaning our guides scout the route fresh each day. Nights are spent in cave shelters or tented camps along the gorge, with temperatures regularly falling below -15°C.

Before you go

Best time & what to pack

Best time to go

  • Mid-January to end of February — the only window when the ice sheet is reliably thick enough
  • Early January can still have unstable ice in sections
  • This trek does not run outside deep winter — the "chadar" simply doesn't exist otherwise

What to pack

  • Extreme cold-rated sleeping bag (-20°C or better) and down jacket
  • Waterproof, insulated boots — feet get wet from ice-walking, this is unavoidable
  • Multiple pairs of thermal socks and a way to dry them overnight
  • A full change of dry clothes kept sealed and untouched until camp
  • Sunglasses rated for snow glare — the gorge reflects intense light on clear days
